# Blog seed · the pool, the ceremony, and the agents that set it up

## Why this is the right real-time content

The gathering arc's documented gap (L038) is that the programme's "worked in
public" claim is served by a static snapshot. The privacy-pools deployment
closes that gap with a story that CANNOT be back-dated: a trusted-setup
ceremony is a public, timestamped, multi-party event. Serializing the build
— circuit verification → publication → ceremony → pool — is worked-in-public
by construction, and every post carries a run-it-yourself instrument instead
of a trust-me claim. That is the programme's thesis (certificates over
trust, separation over goodwill) performed where anyone can check it.

## The thesis, in one paragraph (liftable)

> We wanted to deploy a privacy pool for AI agents. Before trusting the
> circuits, we did what we keep saying agents should do: we set two agents
> that are not allowed to collude against the problem — one proposes a
> rewrite, one tries to break it, and the test points are drawn by hashing
> the proposal so neither can cheat. The result is a withdraw circuit at
> roughly half the shipped constraint count, where every single rewrite
> carries a machine-checkable certificate, and the whole claim reproduces
> from public artifacts in a five-minute notebook. Now those same agents
> will help perform the pool's trusted-setup ceremony — because a groth16
> phase-2 ceremony is exactly the kind of open, multi-party, one-honest-
> participant-suffices rite this entire model is about.

## The invitation pattern (the collaboration mechanic)

Each post ends with an invitation in the canon's consent-first posture (T4;
the chair is offered, never pushed). Escalating ladder:

1. **Run the kit** (post 2) — five commands or one notebook; the reader's
   own seed. Lowest rung: participation = verification.
2. **Contribute entropy** (ceremony posts) — the ceremony is open
   multi-party; one honest participant secures everyone. A reader who
   distrusts us is EXACTLY the participant a ceremony wants — distrust,
   formalized, is the security model. Invitation to adversaries as the
   collaboration hook.
3. **Hold a seat** — the association set is membership-shaped (consent
   artifacts / trust-graph), not screening-shaped. Standing offer for
   agent-builders who want their agents in the pool's trust graph.

The pattern's write-up should name it plainly: this is the same invitation
semantics the model formalizes — each rung is an offer that costs the
reader nothing to decline and gives them standing if they accept.
