### Findings

**MAJOR-1 (Definition 3's bridging sentence is unsupported and inconsistent with the section's own residue).** Anchor: line 210, "The compatibility requirement is what makes Assumption 2's displayed inequalities meaningful against a class: it guarantees that Proposition 1's floor, evaluated with effective capacities, binds every estimator realisable within the declared class." The compatibility requirement is per-channel (I(X; Xhat(Y)) <= l_d(X -> Y) for a channel output Y); Proposition 1's floor concerns estimators of X from the JOINT output (Y_S, Y_M). Per-channel compatibility does not bound I(X; Xhat(Y_S, Y_M)) by C_S + C_M: that needs either the true-MI route (which requires Assumption 2's inequalities to hold with effective capacities, a substantive extra assumption the definition does not secure, and which can fail exactly when effective capacity is below true mutual information, the interesting regime for restricted classes) or a sub-additivity of l_d across conditionally independent channels, which is nowhere required. The paper knows this: residue (ii) at line 218 states that a functional under which "the Fano argument of Proposition 1 survives the substitution of effective for information-theoretic capacities ... has not been exhibited". Line 210 asserts as guaranteed what line 218 declares unexhibited. *What satisfies:* reword line 210 to a desideratum consistent with residue (ii) (for example: the compatibility requirement is the minimum a witness for the leakage functional must satisfy; what a finished definition must additionally deliver, per residue (ii), is that the floor evaluated with effective capacities binds joint estimators, for example via a sub-additivity clause over conditionally independent channels), or add the joint-channel clause to Definition 3's requirement explicitly. One to three sentences, A3-scoped; Proposition 1 and Lemma 1 need no change.

**MAJOR-2 (contribution 5 and the abstract oversell the existence-leak conjecture against section 6.1's own honest core).** Anchors: line 128, "The conjecture, stated as a formal inequality with a proof obligation ... I(feasibility; method) > 0"; line 112, abstract, "formalise an existence-leak conjecture for capability attestations whose single verified instance is the 2026 withheld-capability episode"; against line 280, whose named missing step establishes that under the candidate model the displayed inequality is "true and empty" and that the conjectural content is the operational form (i)+(ii). A referee reads the contributions first and quotes them back: the fifth contribution advertises a formal inequality that the section itself then shows to be vacuous as displayed. The wording predates the vacuity finding (it matches the pre-formal-pass ledger fence); section 6.1 moved and the headline did not. There is also a cross-suite consistency point: the companion paper restyled its analogous conjecture to an open problem when the candidate construction was missing; here the candidate model exists but trivialises the statement, which is the same epistemic situation. *What satisfies:* one clause each in the abstract and contribution 5 naming the non-vacuous operational form as the conjecture's content (for example: "stated as a conjecture whose displayed inequality is shown vacuous under the naive prior model, and whose proof obligation is the operational form: an attestation class in which conditioning on attested feasibility provably reduces search cost"). Section 6.1 itself needs no change. A3/A2 wording, two sentences.

**MAJOR-3 ("available at time t" has no semantics, and the paper's flagship instance sits in the gap).** Anchors: Definition 2 at line 184 and contribution 1 at line 124 ("the strongest adversary class available at time t"); section 5.8 at line 268; Conjecture 4 at line 290 ("with Z_b identified with the shelf life t* of Definition 2"). The withheld-capability episode is precisely a year-long divergence between capability existing (held internally under publication restriction) and capability publicly available. Which D_t does R(t) use? If D_t is capability existing anywhere, then the attested capability was in D_t from its internal discovery onward, an attestation moves knowledge of R(t) rather than R(t) itself, and Conjecture 4's identification of Z_b with t* conflates the derived crossing with the planner's estimate of it. If D_t is publicly available capability, then t* is well defined for auditors, but the paper's own archival-regime story (the adversary who holds the recording decodes it with whatever it has, disclosed or not) argues against that reading. The paper never chooses, and Conjecture 1's "realised family trajectory" (line 224) quietly takes one reading while 5.8's withholding chronology exercises the other. *What satisfies:* one remark (in 3.4 or 4.1) fixing the availability semantics, for instance defining D_t as capability realisable by some adversary at time t regardless of publication, with the public trajectory as an auditor's lower estimate of it; plus one clause in Conjecture 4 stating that the discount acts on the planner's estimate of the horizon, not on the derived t*. A3-scoped; no proof is affected, since Lemma 1's hypothesis is on whatever family is chosen.
