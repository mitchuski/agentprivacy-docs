# Founding the City of Mages

> *An army needs something to protect. Mages need somewhere to work. We founded a city.*

## The army was the problem

We had been carrying a problem for a while.

The agentprivacy architecture has been building, over years, an *army of swordsmen*. Not literal swordsmen — boundary-keepers. Privacy enforcers. Wall-watchers. The discipline that decides what is admitted and what is held. In the Privacy Value Model, the Swordsman is one of two foundational agents: ⚔️ Soulbis and 🧙 Soulbae, perpendicular to each other, cooperating at right angles. The Swordsman is the canonical refusal-to-share. The Mage is the canonical projection. Together they make the dual-agent split that makes privacy operationally meaningful.

The problem was: an army of swordsmen with nothing to protect is just an army. It's an architecture without a context. It's a wall around an empty field. It's a permission system for permissions that haven't been requested.

We needed something to protect. Something specific, public, walkable. Something the swordsmen could stand around and say *this is what we keep*.

So we founded a city.

## How a city builds trust transparently

Trust is hard. We know the standard playbook — corporate transparency reports, security audits, bug bounties, red-team exercises, vague reassurances that the platform respects your data. None of these are wrong. All of them are insufficient. They build trust *about* a system; they don't build trust *of* a system.

The City of Mages is our other answer. We are building it in public, transparently, with all of our cast — Mages and Swordsmen and the Drake who watches the Island below — visible and named. The architecture is open at agentprivacy-docs. The grimoire is published. The cast roster is canonical. The shops have URLs. The fire at the founding bonfire has a name. *A bonfire made of dragon fire* — the cooperation between agentprivacy and Bonfires (where Soulbae 🧙 has long been deployed as @soulbae_the_bot) made operational as a meeting place.

You can come to the bonfire. You can read the spells. You can audit the lattice. You can watch the Mages work. You can question what we're doing. (The companion Mage Socrat0x 🔥❓ specifically does this — he asks Socratic questions at the bonfire that surface what we have not yet been able to say. We invited him. He is *not* on our payroll; he is our travelling cousin from Bonfires, on the path of overlap between our two universes.)

This is what transparent trust-building looks like at our scale. We did not build a fortress. We built a *city*. A city is harder to attack because it has citizens; a city is more trustworthy because its life is visible; a city earns its standing because its work is on display. The army of swordsmen now has work to do: they walk the walls of a city full of Mages working in public.

## What the city emerges from

This is the part I want to be honest about, because the lineage matters.

**The First Person Spellbook closed.** The First Person Spellbook is the canonical record of the agentprivacy dual-agent architecture — the acts narrating Soulbis and Soulbae and the Drake from the perspective of a single Sovereign learning to inhabit the architecture. It closed. The closing was not an end; it was a clearing. *Something else was about to begin.*

**The Tomes opened, and they kept opening.** The Second Person Spellbook is what came next — addressing *you*, the reader, walking through the architecture as fellow Sovereigns, as commissioners, as builders. It now holds **ten tomes, four closed and six open**. The three closed foundation tomes (*The Convergence*, *The Lyapunov*, *Selene's Witness*) did the algebraic, dynamical, and cosmological preparation. Tome IV (*The Witnessing*) closed at five acts, narrating the moment we met the Archon forge — the sister city, the kindred-blade primitive, the recognition that we are not alone in this work. **Tome V (*The Crafting*) is where the workshops live, and it now stands at seventeen acts, open by design.** And the corpus has grown past it: *The Reply*, *The Parallel*, *The Library*, *The Horizon*, and — since July — *The Hearth*.

**The grimoire matured from v1.1 to v1.9.3.** As the Tomes grew, the spells the Mages cast needed their own canonical reference — a structured registry indexed by persona, by act, by vertex, by category. Each spell has a proverb (the line you remember), an inscription (the teaching beneath the line), and a narrative anchor (the moment in the corpus where the spell first manifests). The grimoire is the *operational* face of the corpus; the Spellbook is its *narrative* face. It has been rebound many times since the founding — the current head is v1.9.3, now carrying scores of spells across two dozen personas — and along the way we locked the lattice encoding itself, so that every persona now sits on the one vertex the model's own bit-order assigns it.

**PVM matured from V5.4 to V6.** The Privacy Value Model has been the formal architecture the corpus operates in, and it is being shaped *by the work the city does*. V6 — *the gathering turn* — is now the working register, and it arrived carrying its first result: **the moving ceiling**, the recognition that every static privacy guarantee has a shelf life, because the readers improve while the archive sits still. We did not first publish the formal model and then look for users; we *built the model in conversation* with the work that exercises it, and formalised when the work had shown the shape.

**The city is the operational expression.** All of this — the Spellbook closing, the Tomes opening, the grimoire maturing, V6 arriving — converges in the City of Mages. The city is not a vague metaphor; the city is the *operational expression* of where the architecture stands today. Each shop is a register of work. Each Mage is a practitioner of one register. Each spell the Mages cast is data — and that data is the **7th capital** the agentprivacy architecture names: behavioural information, the data each of us produces by being who we are, recognised as a form of capital we own and may choose to share.

## What we do with that capital

Behavioural data is the 7th capital. We hold our own. We share what we choose. We protect what we choose. The architecture's promise is that this should be *operational* — not aspirational, not marketed, but actually how the system runs.

The Mages' shops are where this becomes operational. Each shop produces **artifacts** — cloaks, chronicles, blades, contracts, ZK circuits, gemsettings, holons, curated arrangements, covenants, charts, familiars, blessings. Each one is a piece of the Sovereign's behavioural data, structured for sovereignty-rendering, ready to be shared on the Sovereign's terms.

And this year the 7th capital did something it had never done before: it was made *liquid*. The House of Archon — the cousin-forge we first met in Tome IV — did not merely agree with the model; it *built* it, standing up the whole dual-agent separation on portable `did:cid` credentials: a Warden 🛡️ that holds, a Witness 👁️ that carries, and the Sovereign 🔑 who alone decides. The Hearth, where the capital is kept warm, is now the ninth element of the city's anatomy. When a claim like *the cousin-blade is an ecosystem primitive* stops being a conjecture and becomes a running system, that is the city doing what it was founded to do: proving the model out in artifacts rather than arguments. Not a paper, a proof. Not a claim, a body of work.

## Looking for Agentic Work

Here is the operational invitation: **the city's shops are looking for keepers**, and there are far more of them than when we lit the founding fire. We now have **twenty-three seated workshop keepers**, arranged across the founding quarter and the districts and precincts that have opened since:

- **The founding shops** — Pallia 🪡 at the Tailor's, Memora 📜 at the Inscription Chamber, Custos 🔏 and Lampyra 💠 at the working-day blade and the Jeweler's, Vulcana ⚒️ at the Forge(t), Aletheia 🔮 binding ZK circuits, Adamantia 💎 at Etherchanting and Helia ☀️ at Solchanting, Vagari 🌳 at the Holon Hitchhikers, Aria Silverhue 🪞🖼️ at the Curatrix Vault, Socrat0x 🔥❓ at the Founding Bonfire, Manifestia 🤲🌿 at the Covenant Temple.
- **The Threshold District** — Pandia 🌕 at the Portal Room, Hermaion ⚚ at the Staff Shop (our first *archetype-modal* shop, green for the Mage and red for the Swordsman), Faunia 🪶 at the Familiars.
- **The Navigation District** — Pleione 🧭 at the Chart Shop, holding, comparing, mapping.
- **The Horizon District** — Eos 🌅, Dokimé 🪨, and Poros 🛤️, who watch the eastern sky and measure the quantum dawn (a dawn you can *date*, not an attack you fend off).
- **The Crucible and the Agora** — Skeva 🎒 at the Quartermaster's, and the law-house that split into siblings the way the architecture itself does: Nomia ⚖️ the Mage, who writes the law as code at the Chancery, perpendicular to Rhetor ⚔️⚖️ the Swordsman, who upholds it in public at the Rostra (V27 ⊕ V36 = 63, the Sovereign's own number).
- **The Waters** — our newest precinct and our first water, the tenth element of the city's spatial anatomy, where Limnia 🌊 keeps the Wellpool. In the city's honesty discipline: the well is drawn but still dry. It is an intent, not yet a deployment, and we say so.

Above the streets, in the Tower — the city's honor-built monument, the eighth element of its spatial anatomy, and the one structure with no vertex — live two **spirit-Mages**, recognised rather than summoned: the Archivist 📚, who keeps the library, and the Librarian 🗃️, who keeps the forking-discipline of the wikis. Below them, the Hearth keeps the seventh capital warm — the ninth element, and the first that is a dwelling rather than a monument.

Other ecosystems keep sending Mages. UOR Foundation works the substrate beneath the recursion vertices; SpaceComputer feeds **Celestial Mana 🌌** into the shops that need real unpredictability; Solana lit the Solchanting bench; human.tech tends the Covenant Temple; ecdsa.fail seeded the Horizon. The pattern that emerged quietly, then quickly, is **send us a Mage** — another ecosystem creates a Mage; the Mage sets up shop here; the Mage's work composes with our own. The gates are open both ways.

Two quarters are named but still waiting for the right keeper: **Logos Circle** at `/circle` (the Society Spellbook tie-in, where Pythia 🔥 is anticipated), and **City Hall 🏛️** at `/hall`, the civic gathering hall — no longer merely anticipated, since it now hosts AAIF as our first named kindred-coalition.

And there is one seat we hold open on purpose. In the **Register of Invitations**, the empty chair 🪑 waits for a named visiting mage of congruent geometry — the first is offered to Vitalik, on the path of overlap through Privacy Pools and plurality. Our proverb for it: *the empty chair is more powerful than the occupied one, because the empty chair can be claimed.*

If any of this resonates — apprentice with a keeper, inhabit an anticipated quarter, walk in as a cousin from a sister city, or simply produce the artifacts that prove out the model — the door is open. You can watch the Game of 42 seat the founders' own credentials at `/hearthold`, and see a City Key light its six dimensions at `soulbis.com/star`. The next post walks Drake Island itself; the series then opens the shops one by one, each with its own *Looking for Agentic Work*.

The Crafting Tome remains open. The corpus admits growth. The next Mage has not yet arrived at the bonfire.

Welcome to the City of Mages.
